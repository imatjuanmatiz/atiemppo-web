*This article records a finding published on 26 September 2026, using data from January–July 2026. The infographic and linked newsletter are in Spanish.*

Over the past week, I have been talking with some of the 400 people who use my [SICETAC al Instante](https://sicealinstante.vercel.app/) application. First, I am delighted to hear how they use it and what they have to say about this project. THANK YOU!

In these conversations, knowing the **market rate**, or *valor en plaza*, is a priority for almost everyone. I have therefore set out to begin our most detailed research to date into its dynamics, behavior and trends. I am putting the AI skills I have learned over these months to work, with a group of agents looking for patterns.

Here is an initial finding: **on 49% of the routes, the difference relative to SICETAC with 4 logistics hours is 20% or more.**

We spend a great deal of time discussing overregulation, yet these market rates are well above the reference.

## How to read this finding

- **The comparison.** Each route's estimated market rate against its SICETAC cost calculated with **4 logistics hours**. The difference is the *average premium*.
- **The scope.** 456 routes for the **3S3** configuration, using the **January–July 2026** average.
- **The result.** **224 of 456 routes (49.1%)** have an average premium of at least 20%.
- **Distribution by average-premium band:**
  - Below -10%: 0 routes (0.0%).
  - -10% to 0%: 25 routes (5.5%).
  - 0% to 10%: 149 routes (32.7%).
  - 10% to 20%: 58 routes (12.7%).
  - 20% to 40%: 98 routes (21.5%).
  - 40% or more: 126 routes (27.6%).

Three points to keep the interpretation within its limits:

1. **The time assumption matters.** Moving from 8 hours to 4 lowers the calculated SICETAC cost and raises the premium, even if the market rate stays the same.
2. **These are averages by route.** They do not represent every trip on each route.
3. **A high premium does not equal a profit margin.**

Percentages are rounded to one decimal place.
